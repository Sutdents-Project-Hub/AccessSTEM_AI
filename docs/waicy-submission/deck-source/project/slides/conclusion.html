CONCLUSION

A graph tutor that knows what it does not know

What we built

A conversational tutor for high-school linear and quadratic graphs. Learners and teachers choose depth and pacing; every claim is labelled given, derived or unknown.

What the evidence shows

On ten scenarios, the tests exposed hint leaks, unreadable-scale advice and missing questions. Each was fixed and re-tested. Mean scores hid failures that pass counts showed.

What comes next

Teacher review, screen-reader testing and a study with blind and low-vision learners before any claim of classroom benefit.

Why it matters: access to a graph should not end at the first description. A learner should be able to ask “why?” and get an honest answer, or an honest “I cannot tell.”

All results are from controlled prototype tests; broader effectiveness is untested.
