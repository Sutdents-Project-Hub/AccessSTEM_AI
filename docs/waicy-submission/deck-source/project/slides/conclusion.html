9 · CONCLUSION

A graph tutor that knows what it does not know

What we built

A focused tutor for high-school linear and quadratic graphs: three learning modes, evidence-labelled answers, and hints that hold until the learner asks for the solution.

What testing changed

Five prompt versions and ten recorded failures: leaked answers, wrong setting names, modes that felt alike. Each fix was re-tested on its cases.

What it could change

A student who cannot rely on sight could ask “why?”, choose how much to hear, and see which facts are given, derived or unknown.

Access to a graph should not end at the first description. It should continue until the learner understands, with an honest “I cannot tell” whenever the evidence runs out.

Potential impact, not a measured result. Next: teacher review, screen-reader testing and a study with blind and low-vision learners.

Close on the learner: keep asking, choose the depth, know what is verified.
