MOTIVATION · THE PROBLEM

A graph is a picture. Access is not understanding.

2.2 billion

people have a vision impairment or blindness worldwide. [1]

43.3 million

people were blind in 2020, projected to reach 61.0 million by 2050. [2]

547,000

US children under 18 with severe vision difficulty (Census 2020, as cited in Doore et al.). [3]

94.8%

of the top 1,000,000 home pages had detectable WCAG 2 failures in 2025. [4]

STEM runs on graphs. When a graph is provided at all, access often comes through “literalized modes (e.g., braille, verbal description)” that “do not directly convey spatial information.” [3]

A learner can receive every number and still miss the shape: where the curve turns, why, and what happens next.

How to read these numbers. They show the scale of vision impairment and of inaccessible digital content. They are not an estimate of how many students would use AccessSTEM AI.

Sources [1]–[4] are listed in the references.

Be precise: 2.2 billion includes all vision impairment (WHO 2019). 43.3 million counts blindness only (Lancet Global Health, GBD 2019, base year 2020). 547,000 is the figure Doore et al. cite from the US Census Bureau for children under 18 with severe vision difficulty. 94.8% is a web-wide accessibility measure, used here only to show that accessible content is not the default.
