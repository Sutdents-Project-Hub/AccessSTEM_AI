How to read these numbers. They describe vision impairment, schooling and assistive-technology access worldwide. They are not an estimate of our users, and the UNICEF figure covers all disability types, not vision alone.

International sources [1]–[4] and [9]–[10]; full references at the end.

Be precise. 2.2 billion is WHO’s count of near or distance vision impairment (fact sheet of 10 February 2026). 43.3 million is a modelled GBD 2019 estimate for blindness in 2020, not a census. The UNICEF analysis uses internationally comparable data from 42 countries and covers all disability types; its education findings are relative to children without disabilities. The WHO and UNICEF report says nearly one billion of the 2.5 billion people who need assistive products are denied access, with coverage from 3% in poorer to 90% in wealthier countries in a 35-country analysis. The WebAIM figure is an automated audit and says nothing about conformance on pages without detected errors.
