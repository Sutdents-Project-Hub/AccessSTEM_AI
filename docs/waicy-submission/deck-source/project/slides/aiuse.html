3 · MOTIVATION · AI IN LEARNING, FROM INTERNATIONAL SURVEYS

AI is already in classrooms. Guidance is not.

54%

of US teens say they used chatbots for help with schoolwork (1,458 teens, fieldwork September–October 2025). [6]

1 in 3

teachers report having used AI in their work, on average across OECD systems: about 75% in Singapore and the UAE, under 20% in France and Japan. [5]

86%

of 3,800+ higher-education students in 16 countries regularly use AI in their studies; over half expect over-reliance to hurt performance. [7]

< 10%

of 450+ schools and universities in a UNESCO survey had formal guidance on generative AI (May 2023). [8]

What this means for us. Learners and teachers already use chatbots, yet three in four teachers report lacking the knowledge or skills to teach with AI [5], and 58% of students feel their AI skills are insufficient [7]. A graph tutor needs explicit teaching rules, not just access.

What blind and low-vision users report. In a two-week diary study with 16 participants, an AI description app averaged 2.76/5 for satisfaction and 2.43/4 for trust [12]. Interviews with 26 blind users describe how they check AI errors [13].

Surveys differ in population and date: [6] US teens, [5] teachers in 55 systems, [7] higher-education students, [8] institutions.

Read each number with its population. Pew is a probability-based survey of US teens aged 13 to 17 (Ipsos KnowledgePanel). TALIS 2024 covers lower-secondary teachers in 55 education systems; the OECD average is about one in three. The Digital Education Council survey covers higher-education students, not school pupils, and its full report is gated; we quote the figures on its published summary page. UNESCO’s survey is from May 2023 and about 20% of respondents were unsure whether their institution had a policy. The two blind and low-vision studies are small qualitative or diary studies: the CHI 2024 study used an app the researchers built, not a commercial product.
