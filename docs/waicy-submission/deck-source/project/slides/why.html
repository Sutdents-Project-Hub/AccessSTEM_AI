Why we built AccessSTEM AI

In math and science, graphs are essential tools for understanding abstract ideas. So we began to ask:

If a student cannot see a graph clearly, how can they understand how a curve changes, how coordinates relate, and what the graph really means?

Knowing is not understanding

Assistive technology can turn parts of an image into text or speech. But knowing what is on a graph is not the same as understanding what it expresses.

Learners differ

Some students need a complete, structured explanation. Some only need key hints. Others build understanding step by step by asking again.

Ask, adjust, build

With a large language model, blind and low-vision students can ask their own questions, adjust the explanation and build math concepts through hints, not just receive a description.

Answers to rely on

The tutor should clearly separate given information, mathematical deductions and what is still unconfirmed, so wrong explanations do not mislead learning.

Our hope: graphs that can be understood, not only read, and every student exploring STEM knowledge in their own way.
