AccessSTEM AI helps blind and low-vision high-school learners, and the teachers who support them, read and reason about linear and quadratic function graphs. The workspace pairs a graph with a written overview, a spoken-form formula, and a table of key points. Learners choose Full guide, Quick summary, or Teacher guide and control when to see the answer.

The tutor uses supplied graph facts and separates given, derived, and unknown information. It asks for missing evidence when a scale or unit cannot be read. In learner Hints only mode, it offers one step and preserves the target result.

We compared four prompt versions across ten English scenarios using real OpenAI responses. The English tests exposed a teacher guide that had collapsed to a single hint. A first revision recovered the draft but added too many questions in a focused rate task. The next revision preserved the three-question draft and honored the teacher's request for a single evidence question in targeted retests.

These are controlled tests with synthetic scenarios. The three lessons await teacher review, and screen-reader use and classroom outcomes require further study.
